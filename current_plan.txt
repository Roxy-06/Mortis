MORTIS — CURRENT STATE AND ROBUSTNESS ROADMAP
Last reviewed: 2026-10-13

PURPOSE
-------
This document describes what the current local prototype really does and a
staged plan for evolving it toward a robust, multi-agent autonomous Bitcoin
paper-trading system. It is a plan, not authorization to place live orders.

The goal is reliable, measurable decision-making under strict risk controls.
Profitability is uncertain and cannot be promised. No stage should be described
as "profitable" unless results have been independently reproduced on unseen
data and then supported by sufficiently long live paper-trading evidence.


1. WHAT THE CURRENT APP DOES
----------------------------

Market data source:
  - Mortis requests public Binance market-data REST endpoints. Its configured
    symbol is BTCUSDT; this is not Delta Exchange data.
  - For strategy analysis, it requests up to 300 candles each for 4h, 1h, and
    15m intervals, and removes candles that have not closed yet. These windows
    therefore include historical candles as well as the latest completed
    candles. The strategy uses the completed candles to calculate indicators;
    it does not simply predict from a chart screenshot.
  - It also requests the latest BTCUSDT ticker price. This is a REST snapshot,
    not a continuous tick-by-tick stream.
  - The screenshot supplied with the request shows Delta Exchange India
    BTCUSD futures, including a 5-minute chart and a 200x leverage selection.
    Mortis does not read that screenshot, connect to the Delta interface, or
    trade that contract. BTCUSD futures and Binance BTCUSDT are different
    instruments/markets and can have different prices, funding, and liquidity.

Timing and decisions:
  - When autonomous mode is enabled, the Python backend starts the trading
    worker. It evaluates the strategy approximately every 15 minutes.
  - Between evaluations, ordinary code polls the Binance ticker approximately
    every 5 seconds to settle an open simulated position. It can miss price
    movements between polls; it is not millisecond or microsecond trading.
  - The strategy is based on closed 4h/1h/15m candles, so the newest candle's
    information can be up to one interval old. A fresh ticker price does not
    make the candle indicators equally fresh.
  - Gemini may propose a direction and rationale from the snapshot. If Gemini
    is unavailable or returns invalid output, deterministic TA rules are used.
    Deterministic code validates the result and calculates risk and sizing.
  - Although multi-agent/news/consensus ideas appear in older project
    documentation, the current automated strategy is not yet a functioning
    multi-agent committee. Do not treat dashboard news cards as automated
    strategy inputs unless code explicitly wires and validates them.

Learning and the ledger:
  - The current app does not train or fine-tune Gemini model weights. It logs
    decisions and simulated trade results, which can support later evaluation.
  - The SQLite SHA-256 event chain is a local tamper-evident audit mechanism;
    it is not a public blockchain or an independently hosted immutable record.
  - Paper positions and returns are simulations. The configured 3% fee and
    slippage assumptions must be checked against the intended venue and
    contract before interpreting simulated performance.


2. TARGET OPERATING PRINCIPLES
------------------------------
  1. Paper trading only throughout this roadmap. Do not add real exchange
     execution as an assumed next step.
  2. Fail closed: stale, missing, malformed, inconsistent, or delayed data
     must lead to "no trade" and a visible, auditable reason.
  3. Separate signal generation from risk approval and simulated execution.
     No language model may bypass deterministic limits or directly submit an
     exchange order.
  4. Use exact market identity everywhere: venue, instrument/contract,
     quote currency, candle interval, and timestamp. Never silently mix
     BTCUSD futures with BTCUSDT spot.
  5. Measure outcomes after fees, slippage, funding where applicable, and
     realistic execution assumptions. Compare against simple baselines.
  6. Treat confidence as an uncalibrated score until tests demonstrate
     calibration. It is not a probability of winning.
  7. Preserve human-operable safety controls even when routine analysis and
     paper execution are autonomous: pause, kill switch, limits, and recovery.


3. STAGED PLAN OF ACTION
------------------------

STAGE A — Establish a trustworthy baseline
  - Freeze and version the current rules, configuration, dependencies, and
    data-source/instrument definitions.
  - Verify data timestamps, candle closure, missing intervals, duplicate
    records, outliers, REST errors, and source failover behavior.
  - Store enough provenance with every decision to reproduce it: data source,
    symbol, candle timestamps, indicator inputs, strategy/config version,
    model/provider/version, output, risk decision, and simulated fills.
  - Add an explicit stale-data threshold and halt trading decisions when it is
    exceeded. Make the dashboard distinguish last market-data update from last
    strategy evaluation.
  - Confirm the fee, slippage, and liquidation assumptions. The current 3%
    round-trip fee is a simulation setting, not a verified Delta fee schedule.

EXIT GATE:
  Repeated tests demonstrate that bad or stale inputs cannot create a trade,
  that a decision can be reproduced from its recorded inputs, and that all
  displayed timestamps and market identifiers are unambiguous.

STAGE B — Build realistic historical evaluation
  - Create a versioned historical dataset for the exact market/instrument the
    simulator represents. Include candles and, if pursuing short-horizon
    execution, trades/order-book data at the resolution actually available.
  - Avoid look-ahead and survivorship leakage. Split data chronologically;
    keep final periods untouched until model/strategy choices are frozen.
  - Model fees, spread, slippage, missed fills, latency, funding, and margin
    rules conservatively. Test adverse conditions and gaps, not just average
    conditions.
  - Evaluate against buy-and-hold, no-trade/cash, and the current strategy.
    Report net return, drawdown, volatility, risk-adjusted return, turnover,
    exposure, win/loss distribution, tail loss, and results by regime.
  - Run walk-forward evaluation over multiple market regimes. Treat one
    favorable backtest or a high win rate alone as insufficient evidence.

EXIT GATE:
  Reproducible out-of-sample results beat an explicitly selected baseline on
  predeclared risk-adjusted criteria after conservative costs, without
  unacceptable drawdown or dependence on one narrow time period. Otherwise
  revise or reject the candidate.

STAGE C — Make evaluation continuous before changing model weights
  - Build an outcome pipeline that joins each decision to the eventual
    simulated result and the market conditions known at decision time.
  - Track confidence calibration with reliability plots and metrics such as
    Brier score or log loss where the target definition supports them.
    Measure calibration separately by confidence band, direction, and market
    regime; do not treat LLM self-reported confidence as truth.
  - Track drift in data quality, feature distributions, prediction quality,
    execution assumptions, and strategy results. Alert and pause when data or
    performance moves outside defined limits.
  - Keep immutable/versioned evaluation sets and retain failed decisions and
    no-trade decisions to avoid selection bias.

EXIT GATE:
  Every reported metric is reproducible from recorded data; dashboards show
  costs, uncertainty, sample size, and drawdown rather than only wins or
  headline return.

STAGE D — Add specialist agents with bounded responsibilities
  - Data-quality agent: reports source freshness, completeness, and anomalies;
    it can veto but cannot create a trade.
  - Technical/regime agents: independently analyze defined features and
    return schema-validated signals with evidence and version identifiers.
  - Optional news/fundamental agent: use licensed/reliable timestamped sources;
    measure whether this input adds out-of-sample value before relying on it.
  - Lead/consensus agent: combine validated signals using a documented,
    testable policy; handle disagreement by abstaining, not by inventing
    certainty.
  - Deterministic risk gate: sole authority for size, leverage, exposure,
    daily loss, fee-aware expected return, and trade eligibility.
  - Simulator/execution agent: simulate orders only, record intended versus
    achieved fills, and cannot loosen risk rules.
  - Compare each agent alone and in combinations using the same frozen
    evaluation periods. More agents are not automatically better.

EXIT GATE:
  Each component has tests, a narrow contract, observable decisions, and a
  measurable contribution. Agent failure, timeout, or disagreement results in
  a safe abstention.

STAGE E — Improve the strategy through controlled experiments
  - Begin with transparent rule/feature improvements and conventional
    supervised models before considering fine-tuning or reinforcement
    learning. Define labels and prediction horizons before training.
  - Candidate features may include multi-timeframe returns, volatility,
    trend, volume, spread, order-book imbalance, and funding, but only when
    historical values are available without future leakage.
  - Use walk-forward training and validation, version datasets/features/code,
    and log experiment parameters and artifacts. Never tune against the
    untouched final evaluation period.
  - Compare candidate signals against the existing baseline under identical
    costs and risk limits. Keep or roll back changes based on predeclared
    out-of-sample gates, not anecdotal wins.
  - If confidence scores are used for sizing, calibrate them on separate data
    and cap their impact. Risk limits remain deterministic and conservative.

EXIT GATE:
  The candidate produces a repeatable improvement on unseen periods, remains
  within drawdown and exposure limits, and does not regress critical stress
  scenarios. Otherwise retain the baseline.

STAGE F — Validate live-data behavior in shadow and paper modes
  - Shadow mode: consume the intended live feed and generate/log decisions
    without opening even simulated positions. Compare timing and signals with
    expected behavior.
  - Paper mode: enable simulated positions only after shadow checks pass.
    Keep a single process responsible for the account to avoid duplicate
    workers or concurrent opens.
  - Add robust reconnect/backoff, rate-limit handling, clock synchronization,
    health checks, structured logs, alerts, and graceful shutdown/restart
    tests. Make outages visible; never silently substitute stale prices.
  - Use streaming/WebSocket market data only if the chosen venue provides a
    reliable documented feed and the app validates sequence, timestamps,
    reconnects, and gaps. Streaming can reduce delay but does not guarantee
    fills or make the system HFT.
  - Run for a predeclared observation period covering varied conditions.
    Compare expected and simulated fills and reconcile account/ledger totals.

EXIT GATE:
  No unexplained missing data, duplicate positions, ledger inconsistencies,
  silent fallback, or unbounded loss in the paper environment throughout the
  observation period.

STAGE G — Operational readiness review
  - Maintain explicit hard limits for leverage, notional, margin, open
    positions, daily loss, and maximum stale-data age; test each limit.
  - Provide a one-action pause/stop control, restart-safe position recovery,
    backups for the SQLite database, and a tested restore procedure.
  - Keep API keys out of source control and logs. Use least-privilege
    credentials; do not configure exchange trading permissions for this
    paper-trading system.
  - Review security, dependency updates, monitoring, incident handling, and
    operator visibility before making the app reachable beyond localhost.
  - Require an explicit separate decision and new safety/security review
    before any future real-money exchange integration. It is not part of this
    plan and must not be enabled by changing a simple environment flag.

EXIT GATE:
  Operational, data-quality, risk, security, and audit checklists pass; a
  human operator can understand and stop the system; unresolved critical
  issues block progression.


4. SUCCESS MEASURES
-------------------
Judge progress using evidence, not the number of agents or the number of
trades:
  - Data freshness/completeness and outage recovery.
  - Reproducibility of decisions and ledger integrity.
  - Net out-of-sample performance against predeclared baselines.
  - Maximum drawdown, tail risk, exposure, and cost sensitivity.
  - Confidence calibration and performance by regime.
  - Simulated fill quality and difference between expected and realized costs.
  - Rate of safe abstention during disagreement, stale data, and service
    failures.
  - Stability over time and successful pause, restart, backup, and recovery.

Do not optimize only win rate, raw return, or agent confidence. Do not promote
a strategy solely because it performed well on the data used to design it.


5. IMPORTANT INTERPRETATION
----------------------------
The current app uses live public market-data snapshots plus recent completed
historical candles; it does not consume the Delta chart in the screenshot and
does not observe every Bitcoin price movement. Strategy decisions are made on
a 15-minute cadence, while paper-position marks are polled about every
5 seconds. This is a local paper-trading prototype, not a trained self-improving
model or an autonomous real-exchange trading system.

The intended path to "improvement" is: collect auditable outcomes, evaluate
against realistic baselines, identify measurable deficiencies, train or adjust
one candidate at a time, and promote only after out-of-sample and shadow/paper
gates pass. No approach guarantees profit.
